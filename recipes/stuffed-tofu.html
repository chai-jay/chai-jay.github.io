Hakka Stuffed Tofu

Ingredients

  • 3 containers of House Tofu brand's firm tofu (it must be this brand and firmness)
  • Salt
  • 3-5 tbsp cooking oil
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • Dash of oyster sauce
  • Dash of dark soy sauce

  • Meat Filling:

    • 10 oz pork loin
    • 1/4 tsp salt
    • 1/4 tsp sugar
    • 1/2 tsp dark soy sauce
    • 1/2 tsp regular soy sauce
    • 1/2 tsp sweet soy sauce
    • 1/2 tsp Oyster sauce
    • Dash of white pepper
    • --
    • 1 tsp corn starch
    • 1/2 tsp sesame oil
    • 1 garlic clove
    • 1 small shallot
    • 1/4 of one jicama (Sah Koh in Cantonese 沙葛)
    • 2 large shrimp
    • --
    • 1 egg
    • 1/2 scallion
    • (Optional) Dash of cumin seeds

Prep Steps

  1. Day before cooking, do these prep steps!

  2. Meat Filling Prep Day 1

    1. Chop up the jicama, shallot, and garlic. Then set aside. Give the jicama a good chop because it helps to give the meat filling a softer and more loose texture.
    2. Chop up each shrimp roughly and set aside. We still want to have small shrimp chunks in the meat filling.
    3. Chop up pork loin until it's pork mince. We're looking for a ground-meat consistency. You can also use premade pork mince from supermarkets but mom doesn't trust that they wouldn't use much of the filler stuff, like skin and off cuts.
    4. In a container, add the pork mince along with the salt, sugar, dark soy sauce, regular soy sauce, sweet soy sauce, oyster sauce, and white pepper.
    5. Keep mixing and folding the meat with your hands for 2-3 minutes until evenly mixed. We're looking to season the pork with the salty ingredients uniformly before the next step.
    6. Add corn starch, sesame oil, garlic, shallots, jicama, and shrimp. Do another round of mixing/folding.
    7. Leave in the fridge covered overnight.

  3. Tofu Prep Day 1

    1. Drain each container of tofu.
    2. Take one tofu block out.
    3. Rinse all sides under tap water.
    4. Dust the top and bottom of the tofu block with salt.
    5. Rub the salt all over each of the 6 sides gently.
    6. Place on a colander.
    7. Repeat for every tofu block.
    8. Place colander in the fridge overnight to slightly dehydrate and season. The tofu will drip water so place a bowl or something under the colander.
  4. The next day, do these next step.

  5. Meat Filling Prep Day 2

    1. Beat 1 egg and add 1/2 of the beaten egg to the meat.
    2. Chop up 1 scallion and add to the meat.
    3. If adding the optional cumin seeds, do a rough chop and add it to the meat.
    4. Mix/fold again for 2 minutes until evenly mixed. It should be a meat paste consistency, like raw dumpling filling.

  6. Tofu Prep Day 2

    1. Get some gloves, a small paring knife, a pair of chopsticks, two plates/bowls, a paper towel, the meat filling, and tofu blocks ready. One plate will be for the finished ready-to-cook stuffed tofu pieces and another plate is for the tofu scraps.
    2. Put on gloves. Place paper towel on the plate where you will put the ready-to-cook stuffed tofu pieces to soak up excess liquid.
    3. Cut each tofu block in half. Either cut lengthwise (into rectangles) or diagonally from corner-to-corner (into triangles) into two tofu pieces. Grandma 外婆 made it with both cuts. Triangle shape is a little bit simpler to cook because it will have one less side to cook and looks prettier according to mom.
    4. With the paring knife, on the "top" side of each tofu piece, draw a thin oval shape. When you're looking at the top side straight-on, the oval shape should be close to the top and bottom edges of the top side (but not too close or it will likely rip when you stuff the filling). The sides of the oval shape should leave a decent amount of space from the left and right edges.
    5. With the knife, carve out the tofu using the oval shape as a guide to make the hole for the filling. Don't go much deeper than halfway into the tofu piece.
    6. With chopsticks, poke two holes from inside the oval filling hole down through the bottom of the tofu piece. You want the holes at about 25% and 75% from the top edge. The holes are more for even cooking when deepfrying but doesn't hurt here.
    7. With chopsticks in one hand and tofu piece in the other, stuff the meat filling into the oval filling hole. Begin with a shrimp chunk in the bottom of the hole (since shrimp cooks quicker than pork). Then fill it up with the meat! Use the thumb of the tofu hand to press down on the meat filling as you fill. You want the meat to overflow and come out of the tofu piece. There won't be much shrinkage from cooking so it'll pretty much be the same size so stuff accordingly.
    8. Clean up meat from the edges if you care about presentation.
    9. Place the ready-to-cook stuffed tofu pieces on the paper-towel-ed plate.

Cooking Steps

  1. Note, there is a fried version of this where you just heat up frying oil and fry until the meat filling reaches 165 degrees F. But we will do a healthier braised version here.
  2. In a pan/wok, heat up cooking oil on high heat until it shimmers. There should be about 1 inch of oil on the bottom of the pan/wok.
  3. Turn the heat to low while you place the tofu pieces so everything cooks evenly.
  4. With one hand, place the stuffed tofu pieces meat-side down. When placing them on the pan, you want to push down a little bit to kinda flatten the meat so they stand up and don't fall over to the side. Start with two pieces in the center and then rotate clockwise/counterclockwise around the pan.
  5. Turn the heat back up to medium high. Cook for about 4-5 minutes or until there is a golden brown crust on the top and the surrounding tofu edges are golden yellow/brown.
  6. Turn the heat back down to low. With wooden chopsticks, flip the tofu pieces on one side in the order you placed them down. Turn the heat back up to medium high or high. Cook for 3-5 minutes or until golden yellow/brown. Note, it's hard to overcook since tofu has so much moisture AND we will also be steaming it after.
  7. Turn the heat back down to low. Flip the tofu pieces to cook the other side. Turn the heat back up to medium high or high. Cook for 3-5 minutes or until golden brown.
  8. (If there's enough oil to cook the bottom side already or if you are cooking triangular shapes, skip this step) Turn the heat back down to low. Flip the tofu pieces to cook the other side. Turn the heat back up to medium high or high. Cook for 3-5 minutes or until golden brown.
  9. Transfer all the pieces into another pan/pot to steam. The outside of the tofu pieces should be golden yellow/brown on all sides at this point. You don't want much (if any) white on the tofu exterior. However it looks at this point will be pretty much how it looks when you plate.
  10. Add about 3-5 tbsp water. The tofu pieces will also leech out some water. The bottom of the pan should have water that is visible when you look in.
  11. Turn the heat to low. Before the water starts to simmer, fry up some garlic in oil until it's slightly burnt. When the water starts to simmer, add in the garlic+oil, dash of oyster sauce, and dash of dark soy sauce. Let it steam for 10-15 minutes or until the center of the meat filling reaches 165 degrees F.
  12. Serve with whole pieces of green leaf lettuce or romaine lettuce to use as wraps for the tofu pieces.
